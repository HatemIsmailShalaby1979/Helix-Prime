Helix Codex OS: everything we know, written down

This is the handover for the team picking up E:\Helix-Prime. It assumes you know nothing about the project. It covers the code, the governance, the release process, and the commercial situation, including the parts that are uncomfortable. Four interactive diagrams carry the structure; this document is the spine that connects them.

00Read this first

Helix Prime is a governed AI operations platform, being commercialised under the name Helix Codex OS. It models the work of a business operations floor: forecasting, scheduling, adherence, onboarding, and quality. Six business engines do the calculation. Nine named AI agents act as department heads. A governance layer sits underneath all of them and refuses to let anything happen without a record of who authorised it.

The engineering is in good shape. 1,758 tests pass in a single uninterrupted run, the linters and security scanners are clean, and the release gate does exactly what it was designed to do. The commercial side is not started. There is no revenue, no pricing, and no sales pipeline. There is one named design partner who has never run a pilot. If you are joining to build, you are joining a well-built engine with no market attached to it yet. That is the honest summary, and the rest of this document supports it.

Three things to know before anything else

1. Seven commits are unpushed, and the repository is still public. The remote origin/main sits at b9d8fb6; HEAD is 7 commits ahead of it and 0 behind, so most of the work is published. Two things still need an owner decision: push the remaining 7 commits, and make the repository private. It is currently public ("private": false), which is the larger exposure of the two.

2. The demo video on disk is stale, and its captions no longer match it. The script was rewritten to remove invented claims and the .vtt caption track was regenerated from it, but assets/Helix_Prime_5Min_Demo.mp4 is an untracked build output dated 2026-08-27 that was never rebuilt. Its narration runs longer than the corrected script in three of five chapters. Rebuild it or withdraw it — and do not ship the two files as a matched pair, because they are not one.

3. "Production ready" is false and the system says so itself. Twenty-one external verification items are open. The production gate returns exit 1 by design. Do not let anyone, inside or outside the team, describe this as production ready.

02For non-technical readers

If your job is marketing, sales, partnerships, or running the business, this section is yours. You do not need to read the rest, though the four diagrams are written to be readable without any engineering background.

What the product actually does

A sports academy, a contact centre, or any operation with staff and shifts has the same recurring problems. How many people do we need on Tuesday afternoon? Who is running late, and does it matter? Is the new hire actually trained, or just scheduled? Helix models those questions as a set of connected engines, then puts an AI agent in charge of each department: operations, HR, marketing, sales, compliance, IT, fraud, learning, and one coordinator above them all.

The part that makes it different from a dashboard is the layer underneath. Every action an agent takes is recorded, attributed, and either approved by a human or refused. The system is built so that it would rather stop working than do something unauthorised. That is a deliberate engineering choice, and it is the reason the production gate refuses to open.

Who the nine agents are

These are the characters in the product story. Each maps to a real role with real approval limits, readable data domains, and KPIs, defined in organization/role-catalog.yaml.

One product, two names

The repository, the license, the domain concepts, and most documentation call it Helix Prime. The commercial and release layer calls it Helix Codex OS, and the release manifest records the product as Helix-Prime-Codex. They are the same thing. Decide which name is the public one and make it consistent, because right now external material uses both.

What you can and cannot say publicly today

Safe to say

  • It is a working, tested platform: 1,758 automated tests in one green run, clean security scans.
  • It is designed to be governed and auditable, and it refuses unsafe actions by default.
  • It has been validated as suitable for a supervised controlled pilot.
  • It is built by an operator with 28 years in the domain, with a public repository.

Do not say

  • Anything implying production readiness.
  • Any customer name other than the design partner, and never imply they pay.
  • Any ROI, savings, or waste-reduction figure. All earlier ones were invented.
  • Any reference to a proof ledger, or to a completed pilot. Neither exists.

04Diagram 1 · The platform landscape

What the system is made of, how the pieces connect, and where the commercial layer sits relative to the governed core. Read this one first if you are new to the codebase.

How to read it

The diagram is organised left to right by trust. On the far left is the outside world: the interfaces and connectors. In the middle sit the six engines and nine agents that do the work. Underneath them is the governed core, which is the part that cannot be bypassed. On the right is the commercial surface, which today is a marketing site and a deck rather than a revenue channel.

Two lines matter more than the boxes. The deployment boundary marks the fact that everything currently runs on one machine, bound to 127.0.0.1. There is no cloud redundancy and no external identity provider. The security boundary wraps the governed core and the audit trail, and it is the reason the product behaves conservatively: a request that cannot be verified is refused rather than allowed through.

For engineers

The governed core is control_plane/ plus the security and audit modules. Engines are reused through existing capability ids; vertical packs such as capabilities/restaurant/ and capabilities/sports_academy/ are consumers, not forks. The reference pattern for any new pack is capabilities/restaurant/, and copying its shape is a hard rule rather than a preference.

06Diagram 3 · The truth chain

How a number travels from a synthetic fixture to a public claim, and where it can be stopped. This is the diagram that explains why the project refuses to overstate itself.

Why this matters more than it looks

Most systems are happy to move a number from a database into a dashboard and then into a sales deck. This one inserts checkpoints. Data arrives from fixtures or connectors, passes a governed intake that classifies it and attaches provenance, gets computed by an engine, lands in durable state that is both memory and audit trail, and only then can it become evidence or a permission. Each hop carries the correlation id, the data mode, the basis, and the source.

The practical consequence: you cannot produce a claim that is not traceable. That is why the project's own commercial document reads as a list of retractions. The system makes dishonesty expensive, and that discipline is worth preserving when you extend it.

The four fields on every governed record

tenant_id, client_id, a provenance block (correlation_id, data_mode, basis, sources), and classification. A record missing the provenance envelope is refused. This is not theoretical: during the 2026-09-18 validation the release gate's own app_memory_store_isolation probe failed because it wrote a record without the envelope. The fix was to the probe, not to the rule.

The data discipline you inherit

  • DATA_MODE = "simulated_realistic". Nothing in the pilot path is real data.
  • production_readiness = "NOT_ESTABLISHED", carried on every record.
  • Connectors are read-only. There are no live writes to any external system.
  • Pilot data must be synthetic or explicitly consented. The data scope is recorded as SYNTHETIC_OR_CONSENTED_ONLY.
  • No autonomous irreversible, financial, personnel, compliance, or external-communication action.

09The rules you must not break

These come from AGENTS.md and 00_CONSTITUTION.md. They exist because breaking them breaks the governed core, and the core is the product's actual differentiator.

  1. Copy the restaurant pack. capabilities/restaurant/ is the pattern for any vertical pack. Do not invent a new shape.
  2. Never edit three files. organization/role-catalog.yaml, control_plane/governance.py (the organisation catalog), and organization/capability-registry.yaml plus its mirrors. Roles stay pack-local. Engines are reused through existing capability ids.
  3. Keep the data discipline. DATA_MODE = "simulated_realistic", read-only connectors, no live writes, production_readiness = "NOT_ESTABLISHED".
  4. Every governed record carries its envelope. Tenant, client, provenance (correlation id, data mode, basis, sources), evidence refs, classification. No envelope, no record.
  5. No comments in code. Module docstrings are kept, mirroring the restaurant pack. Nothing else.
  6. Every step ends the same way. Tests at or above baseline, ruff clean on new files, AGENTS.md updated, commit made.
  7. Commit discipline. Commit after every completed step. Stage only the files you touched, never git add -A. Never commit .db files, __pycache__, or .venv*.
  8. Truth is paramount. From the constitution, and worth quoting in full: "A feature is not verified because an agent, document, or generated JSON file says it is."
The authority chain

00_CONSTITUTION.md → docs/HELIX_CODEX_OS_MASTER_BLUEPRINT.md → implementation. MASTER_STORY.md, ROADMAP.md, CHANGELOG.md, and the status summaries in docs/ are subordinate. When a subordinate record contradicts the constitution or the blueprint, the subordinate record is wrong.
