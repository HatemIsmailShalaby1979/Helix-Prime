## 3. The sports academy business — what you need to know

Since you said you are brand new to this line of business, here is everything you need to understand before you talk to the client. Read this carefully.

### 3.1 How a sports academy actually works

A sports academy is a service business that delivers coaching to athletes (usually children/teens). The revenue comes from monthly fees, camp fees, and sometimes merchandise or events. The core operations are:

1. **Enrollment** — signing up athletes, collecting fees, managing family accounts
2. **Scheduling** — assigning coaches to sessions, booking facilities, avoiding conflicts
3. **Attendance** — tracking who shows up, who doesn't, and who is falling behind
4. **Coaching quality** — ensuring coaches deliver the curriculum, track athlete progress
5. **Retention** — keeping athletes enrolled (churn is the silent killer)
6. **Staff management** — hiring coaches, setting expectations, measuring performance
7. **Parent communication** — keeping parents informed and happy (parents = the payer)
8. **Financial tracking** — revenue, expenses, profitability per program

### 3.2 The typical organizational hierarchy

```
                    Owner / Director
                         |
              +----------+----------+
              |                     |
        Head Coach              Operations Manager
              |                     |
     +--------+--------+      +----+----+----+
     |        |        |      |    |    |    |
  Coach 1  Coach 2  Coach 3  Admin  Finance  Marketing
```

In a small academy (like Scoach Academy Hub likely is), the owner is probably doing all of these roles themselves. That is why they are overwhelmed.

### 3.3 The KPIs that matter for a sports academy

Based on industry research, these are the metrics a well-run sports academy tracks:

**Financial KPIs:**
| KPI | What it measures | Target |
|-----|-------------------|--------|
| Monthly recurring revenue (MRR) | Stable income from memberships | Growing month-over-month |
| Churn rate | % of athletes who leave each month | < 5% monthly |
| Customer lifetime value (LTV) | How much one athlete pays over their time | LTV:CAC > 3:1 |
| Revenue per athlete | Total revenue / number of athletes | Increasing |
| Facility utilization | % of available slots that are booked | > 70% |

**Operational KPIs:**
| KPI | What it measures | Target |
|-----|-------------------|--------|
| Attendance rate | % of enrolled athletes showing up | > 85% |
| Schedule adherence | % of sessions starting/ending on time | > 90% |
| Coach utilization | % of coach hours spent coaching vs idle | > 75% |
| Athlete progression | Skill level improvement over time | Measurable |
| Parent satisfaction | Survey or NPS score | > 40 NPS |

**Staff KPIs:**
| KPI | What it measures | Target |
|-----|-------------------|--------|
| Coach retention | % of coaches who stay year-over-year | > 80% |
| Time-to-competency | How fast a new coach reaches full load | < 30 days |
| Quality score | Curriculum delivery consistency | > 85% |

## 6. How to present the solution (pitch script)

### 6.1 The golden rule

**Do not demo the product. Demo the pain.**

The client doesn't care about your architecture, your 9 agents, or your hash-chained audit ledger. They care about their chaos. Your job in the first meeting is to show them you understand their chaos better than they do — then offer to fix it.

### 6.2 Discovery questions (ask these BEFORE pitching anything)

Print these. Bring them to the meeting. Ask them in this order:

1. "Walk me through a typical day — from when you arrive to when you leave. What takes the most time?"
2. "When a parent calls to ask about their child's progress, what do you do right now to answer that?"
3. "How do you know if a coach is doing a good job? What would you need to see to feel confident?"
4. "When an athlete stops showing up, how long does it take you to notice? What do you do about it?"
5. "If I asked you 'how many athletes do you have right now and what's your monthly revenue' — could you answer in under 30 seconds?"
6. "What's the one thing that, if it disappeared tomorrow, would make your life 50% easier?"
7. "Have you tried any software before? What happened?"

**Do not pitch after these questions.** Say: "Thank you. I now understand your situation much better. Let me come back in a few days with a specific plan for your academy." Then leave.

### 6.3 The pitch (after discovery — meeting 2)

**Structure:** 15 minutes total. No slides. Whiteboard or paper.

**Opening (2 minutes):**
"Based on what you told me, you're running this academy on willpower and memory. You're the CRM, the scheduler, the KPI tracker, and the operations manager — all at once. That's why it feels like a mess. It's not that you're disorganized — it's that you don't have a system to organize into."

**The pain mirror (3 minutes):**
Restate the top 3 pains they mentioned in discovery, in their own words. This proves you listened.

**The offer (5 minutes):**
"I build something called Helix Codex OS. It's an operations system — like having an AI operations manager that never sleeps but always asks you before doing anything. Here's what I'd do for your academy:

1. **Track every coach's attendance and KPIs automatically** — you'll see a dashboard, not guess.
2. **Build athlete and family profiles** — every child's history, attendance, and progress in one place.
3. **Set up role-based access** — your coaches see their sessions, your admin sees billing, you see everything.
4. **Detect when an athlete is about to churn** — before they leave, not after.

I'd like to do this as a **pilot** — free for the first 60 days. You pay nothing until you see value. In exchange, you let me use your academy as a case study (anonymized if you prefer) and give me feedback on what works and what doesn't."

**The close (3 minutes):**
"Here's what I need from you: your top 3 pains ranked, access to your current data (rosters, schedules, anything you have), and 30 minutes a week for a check-in call. That's it. I handle everything else."

**What NOT to say:**
- "I have 9 AI agents with content-based routing" — they don't care
- "It uses Erlang C for forecasting" — they don't know what that is
- "It has a hash-chained audit ledger" — say "every action is logged and auditable"
- "It's local-first with cloud scaffolding" — say "it runs on your computer, your data stays with you"
- "It's pre-pilot with 14/14 controlled-pilot gates passing" — say "I'm offering this as a pilot because I want to prove it works for you before you pay"

## 7. Pricing strategy and negotiation guide

### 7.1 Pricing principles (from research)

Based on solo-founder pricing research (solofoundr.co, 2026):

1. **Price on value, not cost** — if Helix saves the owner 10 hours/week and prevents 5 athletes from churning (each worth $200/month), that's $1,000+/month in value. Price at 20-30% of value created.
2. **Start slightly high, not low** — if 8/10 prospects say yes immediately, you're too cheap. If 2/10 say yes, you're in range.
3. **Never discount for anything except annual prepayment** — discounting trains buyers to negotiate. Offer 2 months free for annual prepay instead.
4. **Two tiers, not five** — keep it simple. "Pilot" and "Full" or "Single location" and "Multi-location."
5. **Raise prices for new customers yearly** — your first client gets a deal. Your second client pays more.

### 7.2 Recommended pricing structure

| Tier | Monthly price | What's included | Target client |
|------|--------------|-----------------|---------------|
| **Pilot** | Free (60 days) | 1 engine (their top pain), 1 location, up to 100 athletes | Scoach Academy Hub (first client) |
| **Starter** | $350/month | 3 engines, 1 location, up to 100 athletes | Small academies |
| **Growth** | $600/month | All 6 engines, 1 location, up to 200 athletes | Growing academies |
| **Scale** | $1,200/month | All 6 engines, multi-location, unlimited athletes | Multi-location / franchise |

**Annual prepay discount:** Pay for 10 months, get 12 (2 months free). This improves your cash flow and locks in the client.

### 7.3 The negotiation conversation

**When:** Day 60 of the pilot (after they've seen value).

**How to frame it:**
"Over the past 60 days, we've set up attendance tracking, built athlete profiles for [N] athletes, and started tracking [N] coach KPIs. Your attendance visibility went from 'I think they show up' to a real-time dashboard. You caught [N] athletes at risk of churning before they left.

Here's what I propose: $350/month for the Starter tier — that covers attendance, CRM, and KPI tracking for up to 100 athletes. If you want the full system — scheduling optimization, churn prediction, automated onboarding — that's $600/month.

I'd recommend starting at $350 for the next 90 days, then reviewing. If you prepay annually, you get two months free — so $3,500/year instead of $4,200.

What matters to you: the monthly number, or the value you get?"

### 7.4 Value justification (the math to show the client)

| What Helix does | Value created | Monthly equivalent |
|-----------------|-------------|-------------------|
| Saves owner 10 hours/week | 10 hrs × $25/hr | $1,000 |
| Prevents 3 athletes churning | 3 × $200/month | $600 |
| Prevents 1 coach leaving | Replacement cost | $500 |
| Reduces billing errors | ~2% of revenue | $200 |
| **Total monthly value** | | **~$2,300** |

At $350-600/month, you're charging 15-26% of the value created. That's a compelling ROI.

### 7.5 What NOT to do

- **Don't price per athlete** — this punishes the client for growing. Use flat tiers.
- **Don't offer a free tier forever** — free users don't give real feedback and don't refer paying customers.
- **Don't negotiate below $300/month** — at that price, you can't sustain the business. Walk away if needed.
- **Don't add custom features for free** — every "can you also add X?" is a scope conversation. "That's a great idea. It's not in the current scope, but I can add it for $X or include it in the Growth tier."

## 9. Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Severity | Mitigation |
|------|----------|------------|
| Client expects a polished SaaS, gets an alpha | High | Be honest upfront: "This is a pilot. The system works, but the interface is functional, not pretty. You're getting something built specifically for you." |
| Client's data is messy or incomplete | High | Data cleaning is part of the pilot. Build an import script. Don't expect clean data. |
| Competitor swoops in with a demo | Medium | Your moat is depth + governance + customization. A competitor can demo faster, but can't adapt to the client's specific needs. |
| Client doesn't adopt the system | High | Train 1-2 key staff first. The owner alone cannot drive adoption. Make the dashboard addictive — they should WANT to check it. |
| You get overwhelmed as a solo builder | High | Scope strictly. One engine at a time. Say "not yet" to feature requests. Don't try to build everything. |
| Client asks for mobile app | Medium | "Not in the pilot scope. The web cockpit works on mobile browsers. Native app is a future roadmap item." |
| Client asks for payment integration | Medium | "Start with manual payment tracking. Payment integration requires compliance (PCI DSS) and is a Phase 2 conversation." |
| Client churns during pilot | Medium | If they churn during a free pilot, the problem is either product or relationship. Ask: "What didn't work?" Listen. Fix. Move on. |
