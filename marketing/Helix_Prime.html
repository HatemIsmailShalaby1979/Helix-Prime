Why this exists

28 years of the same walls.

Forecasting by hand. Feedback caught days late. Talent decisions made without data. Client health discovered after it is already a problem.

I built Helix Prime because operational intelligence should be governed, not autonomous.

  • Decisions have owners. Every recommendation carries a role, authority boundary, and evidence.
  • Actions require approval. Nothing self-deploys; approval enforces segregation of duties.
  • Memory carries provenance. Every governed record is tenant-scoped, evidence-linked, and hash-chained.
  • Improvement is measured. Proposals are evaluated, reviewed, approved, or rolled back — in the open.
"The schedule says you have enough people — but the phones are ringing off the hook." — the problem this system is built to stop.

The difference between a tool and a governed system

Governance is the product.

Every claim above is enforced by code, not by policy documents. These controls are structural facts of the repository — verified by tests.

Approve / deny with segregation of duties

Workflows move through approval state machines. Self-approval is denied; wrong-role reviewers are denied; every transition is appended to an immutable ledger.

Append-only, hash-chained audit chain

audit_events is tamper-evident: each record links to the previous hash, and chain verification is a release gate that fails closed.

Kill switch — fail-closed halt

A persisted /api/halt/* engage/release switch halts submissions, approvals and executions while engaged. Unreadable flag ⇒ engaged. Cancel stays available as the safe direction.

Tenant identity + deny-by-default authorization

Requests are authorized at a single policy seam before reaching storage. Cross-tenant reads are denied; unknown roles, engines and classifications fail closed.

Governed memory with full provenance

Every record carries tenant_id, client_id, correlation_id, data_mode, basis, sources, evidence refs and classification — with flag-based retention that never hard-deletes.

Data discipline

Packs run simulated_realistic data through read-only connectors. Live writes and external connectors are intentionally disabled until the production gates pass.

Local-first by default, cloud-ready by design.

One canonical deployable artifact — the governed API spine — with the cockpit as a read-only secondary surface.

helix-api

The canonical FastAPI spine. Binds 127.0.0.1:8000, exposes identity, RBAC, approvals, kill switch, metrics and the audit chain. Refuses to boot in production without external gate inputs.

The Streamlit dashboard — a read-only diagnostic surface, explicitly secondary. Equivalent to the legacy launch.py launcher.
